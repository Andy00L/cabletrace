# Watched regions

CableTrace records ships near subsea cables in eight regions. Each region is data in one registry
(`src/data/regions/`, typed by `src/lib/regions/region.ts`): its box, time zone, live feed, cable and pipeline sources
with their licences, recording thresholds and seabed blocks. The recorder, the frame collector and the database read
the registry; none of them branches on a region id. This page gives the reasons and numbers behind each region, the
storage and egress budget of the Supabase free plan, and the commands that apply it.

Numbers dated 2026-10-17 come from 40 ship frames of the /live globe (09:11 to 15:41 UTC, every 10 minutes,
`https://cabletrace.vercel.app/api/ship-frames/index.json`; only 6.5 hours were stored, not a full day) unless a line
says otherwise.

**What the numbers say.** aisstream is a network of volunteer shore receivers. It hears the English Channel, the
Danish straits and the North Sea well, and five of the six ranked regions barely: 0 to 1 position a minute near their
cables. Recording there costs almost nothing and catches little until a better feed arrives (BarentsWatch for
Norway, for example). The registry makes that a one-line change per region.

### Cable and pipeline seeds

One migration per region, generated by `scripts/regions/build-cable-seed.ts` from OpenStreetMap (ODbL 1.0) or, for
Perth, where OpenStreetMap maps no submarine cable, ACMA's cable locations (CC BY 4.0, via Geoscience Australia and
AODN). Lines are submarine power cables, telecom cables and oil, gas or supply pipelines; outfalls, intakes and chart
lines of unknown kind are left out. Ways of one cable are joined, clipped to the box plus 0.1 degrees, simplified to
100 m, and lines under 5 km inside the box (harbour crossings) are dropped (`src/data/regions/seed-options.ts`).

Each seed also writes `public.cable_landing_countries` (created by the fault-signal migration `20261019300000`): a line
lands in a country when an end of one of its source ways lies on that country's land or within 2 km of it, by Natural
Earth admin-0 at 1:10m (v5.1.2, public domain, its `ISO_A2_EH` code). Overpass returns whole ways, so ends outside the
box count (C-Lion 1: FI and DE). Lines ending at sea at both ends (platform pipelines) get none; a cable whose
downloaded ways stop short of a landing gets only the landings they reach (Latvia-Sweden 1: LV only).

## Recording: one code path, measured choice

Every minute pg_cron calls `record-vessel-positions`. One run fetches Digitraffic for the regions recorded from it
and, at the same time, listens to aisstream for 40 s with the boxes of the other regions; both go to
`public.ingest_vessel_positions` in one call. The database finds each report's region by its box
(`public.watched_regions`), keeps it only when that region is recorded from the report's feed, matches it to that
region's cables, and thins stationary repeats with the region's thresholds.

The two options the brief named, with the report spacing each gives the detector (`src/lib/detection/drag-rules.ts`
needs a 20-minute run, a reference speed from at least 3 moving reports, and 5 reports for the heading rule):

| Option | Spacing per ship | What the rules get | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The frame collector batches near-cable reports from its 105 s world capture into `ingest_vessel_positions` | at most 2 positions per 10 minutes, then 8 to 9 minutes of silence | a 20-minute run holds about 4 reports: the heading rule (5) and the change point (6) fail; a ship at 8 knots crosses a 6 km corridor in 24 minutes and leaves 2 to 5 positions | nothing new |
| **Chosen:** the minute recorder adds a regional aisstream listen of 40 s | 1 position a minute per ship, as Digitraffic gives the Gulf | the spacing the rules were set on | no new invocation (the recorder already runs every minute); one more aisstream connection for 40 s a minute, 2 of the 3 a key may hold; about 8 messages a second (median 359 ships heard in the seven boxes at the world capture's 0.022 messages per ship a second), against 122 a second that the collector handles in 610 to 640 ms of CPU |

Moving class A ships report every 2 to 10 s and moving class B ships every 30 s (ITU-R M.1371-5, Table 1), so 40 s
hears every moving ship; class A ships at anchor report every 3 minutes, and the thinning keeps one of their
positions per 10 minutes anyway.

Checked against production: 15 minutes of live Digitraffic reports, filtered by the registry and stored through the
migrated `ingest_vessel_positions` (PGlite, 2026-10-17), kept 49.9 positions a minute after the first minute; the
production recorder kept 46.2 a minute over its last hour. The region-aware SQL keeps what the Gulf-only SQL kept.

### The 3 km corridor hides the one real AIS drag on record

The PGlite check replays Yi Peng 3's track (Danish Maritime Authority archive, 18 Nov 2024, 281 rows,
`scripts/backtest/yi-peng-3-track.ts`) through the migrated SQL and the detector with the Central Baltic seed:

| Rows kept | Detector |
| --- | --- |
| all 281 | alert, score 0.7 |
| within 3 km of a cable: 107 | nothing |
| within 5 km: 222 | nothing |
| within 10 km: 275 | alert, score 0.7 |

The speed-deficit rule needs the hour of normal sailing before the run, which happened away from the cables. Every
region, the Gulf included since 17 Oct, records 3 km today. Widening to 10 km multiplies stored positions by 1.7
(Gulf), 1.8 (Channel) and 2.6 (Danish straits) and the detector's reads with them. `candidateCableMetres` (3 km) keeps
the detector's ship list unchanged when the corridor widens: a ship reaches the detector only after a drag-band report
within 3 km of a cable, so 10 km lengthens the same ships' tracks instead of adding ships. This is a decision for the
lead and Andy (below), not a default this change makes.

## Storage inside 500 MB

**Bytes per row.** Production measured about 42 MB a day at 105 positions a minute before thinning
(ambition-pass section 1): 278 bytes a stored row with its three indexes. PGlite gives 181.5 bytes a row (109.3 heap)
on a freshly loaded, vacuumed table; production's random inserts and deletes leave pages part-full, so the budget uses
278.

**Retention.** Each region keeps its positions 72 hours in the database (`hotRetentionHours`), capped at 47 hours for
aisstream, which publishes no terms (the project keeps its data at most 2 days, as for the frames). An hourly
`public.prune_vessel_positions()` enforces both. The Gulf of Finland (Digitraffic, CC BY 4.0) moves every complete UTC
day to a gzip file in the private bucket `position-archive` before the prune drops it (kept 30 days), and every alert's
ship track to a file kept forever (`src/lib/regions/archive-layout.ts`). If archiving fails, the prune still drops
Gulf rows at 14 days, the retention the table had before.

| | Positions a minute | Retention | Rows held | MB at 278 bytes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gulf of Finland | 46.2 | 72 h | 199,584 | 55.5 |
| Seven aisstream regions | 44.3 | 47 h | 124,926 | 34.7 |
| **Hot table** | 90.5 | | 324,510 | **90.2** |
| Gulf alone at the old 14 days | 46.2 | 14 days | 931,392 | 258.9 |
| All eight at 14 days, if the feeds allowed | 90.5 | 14 days | 1,824,480 | 507.2 |

All eight regions at 14 days do not fit 500 MB, hence the archive. With the 10 km corridor the hot table would hold
about 171 MB (Gulf 79.8 a minute, aisstream regions 95.4).

**Storage files (1 GB).** Ship frames: 47 hours of 10-minute frames at about 172 KB each (6.88 MB for the 40 listed)
is about 48 MB. Gulf day files: 20.7 bytes a row compressed (830 real Digitraffic rows recorded through the migrated
SQL in 15 minutes, `.scratch` probe), so a day of 66,528 rows is about 1.4 MB and 30 days about 41 MB.

## Egress inside 5 GB a month

The free plan counts every byte the database and Storage send out. The detector is the largest reader:
`public.detection_inputs()` sent 0.85 MB a call for the Gulf alone (decision log, 17 Oct), 1.22 GB a month at one call
every 30 minutes, of which 73 KB is cable routes.

- **Cables.** Every route of the eight regions as the detector's GeoJSON is 184 KB (PGlite), against 73 KB for the
  Gulf seed. `detection_inputs` now sends only cables within 10 km of a candidate position, clipped to the box around
  those positions grown by 0.25 degrees. On Yi Peng 3's replay that is 12 lines and 2.3 KB instead of 516 lines and
  168 KB, with the same detection (alert 0.7) from both; the anchor path stays within the chain's layback of the
  ship, far inside the clip.
- **Positions.** They scale with candidate rows: 0.76 MB a call for the Gulf's 46.2 a minute; the aisstream regions
  add 44.3 a minute, about 0.73 MB more. Projected: about 1.55 MB a call, 2.2 GB a month at 30 minutes.
- **Collector.** Unchanged: 120 to 230 KB of the newest frame per run, 0.5 to 1.0 GB a month. Cross-source samples
  are inserts, not reads.
- **Recorder.** Inserts and a one-number reply each minute. The daily archive reads one Gulf day as JSON:
  67.3 bytes a row as JSON, about 4.5 MB a day, 134 MB a month.

| Reader | Today | After this change, 3 km | With 10 km |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Detector (every 30 min) | 1.22 GB | about 2.2 GB | about 4.2 GB |
| Detector (every 60 min) | | about 1.1 GB | about 2.1 GB |
| Collector frame reads | 0.5 to 1.0 GB | same | same |
| Archive day read | | about 0.13 GB | about 0.23 GB |

The southern North Sea, measured at 64.6 positions a minute, would add about 1.06 MB a call (1.5 GB a month at 30
minutes) on its own; that is why slot 7 went to the Channel. With the 10 km corridor the budget holds only with the
detector every 60 minutes.

## Edge function limits (supabase.com, read 2026-10-17)

- `supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/limits`: 2 s of CPU per request (async I/O excluded), 256 MB of memory, a worker
  stays up 150 s on the free plan (400 s paid), 150 s request idle timeout, 100 functions per project (free).
- `supabase.com/pricing`, free plan: 500 MB database, 5 GB egress plus 5 GB cached egress, 1 GB file storage, 50 MB
  per upload, 500,000 edge function invocations a month, projects paused after a week without activity.

Invocations a month: recorder 43,200 (every minute), archive 30, collector 4,320, detector 1,440: 48,990, under 10% of
the quota. The recorder run now lasts about 45 s (40 s listen, then one ingest); pg_net waits up to 58 s.

## Cross-source check

The frame collector holds both raw feeds on every run: its 105 s aisstream world capture and the Digitraffic
snapshot for the same frame. For each region recorded from Digitraffic (the Gulf of Finland) it stores one sample in
`public.cross_source_samples`: ships each feed heard, ships in both, ships only aisstream heard, and for ships both
stamped within 2 minutes, the distance between their positions after moving aisstream's report along its course to
Digitraffic's time (`src/lib/live/cross-source.ts`). The frames themselves cannot answer this: inside the Digitraffic
area a frame keeps the Digitraffic report of a ship whenever it has one (`src/lib/live/ship-merge.ts`), so aisstream's
own view of the Gulf never reaches Storage.

`scripts/regions/cross-source.ts` judges the samples of the last hours. Tolerances:

- ships: at least 90% of the moving ships (1 knot or more) aisstream hears in the Gulf are in Digitraffic's snapshot
  (Digitraffic's receivers see about 7 times more ships there);
- places: at least 95% of matched pairs within 250 m (both feeds relay the same transponder; 2 minutes of dead
  reckoning at 20 knots with a 5 degree course error drift about 110 m);
- at least 20 matched pairs, or no verdict.

Exit codes: 0 agree, 1 disagree, 2 could not judge. The same verdict over the last 6 hours is in `/api/health`
(`crossSource`, beside the status, which it does not change) and in the `/live` moment card, where a disagreement is
an alert line read from that cached health answer (`src/lib/health/cross-source-health.ts`,
`src/lib/health/feed-check-report.ts`). The first day disagreed: on 17 Oct 2026, 26 samples gave 80%
of aisstream's Gulf ships in Digitraffic against the 90% target, while 99.9% of 692 matched positions agreed within
250 m. In the newest frame, 11 of the 14 ships only aisstream heard sat at 0 knots in Helsinki and Turku harbours,
which points to the two feeds' scope (moored or small craft), not to bad positions. Since 18 Oct 2026 (Andy's call)
only moving ships are compared and the 90% target stays: the anchor-drag watch only ever needs ships under way.
Samples written before the change age out of the 6-hour status window within 6 hours.

The table has no public policy (`20261019060000_close_public_reads.sql`). `public.cross_source_samples_since(hours)`,
a security definer function capped at 7 days and 1,008 rows (about 150 KB), is callable by the service role only
(`20261019600200_cross_source_server_only.sql`): the health route and the script read it with the secret key, so an
anonymous loop cannot spend the plan's egress on it, and `/api/health` refuses query strings so its CDN cache holds.
`public.watched_regions` and `public.position_archives` have no public read at all.

## Commands for the lead

Apply in this order, after review. Migrations first: the new SQL accepts calls from the deployed recorder (reports
without `source` are read as Digitraffic), while the new recorder needs the new columns.

```bash
# 1. Database: watched regions, the seven seeds with their landing countries, regional recording, the archive.
#    Needs 20261019300000 (fault signals, public.cable_landing_countries) applied first, as it is in production.
bunx supabase db push
#    expected: 20261019400000 to 20261019400700, 20261019500000 and 20261019500100 applied; 336 rows in public.cables;
#    landing rows for 161 of the 320 new lines; jobs record-vessel-positions (58 s timeout), prune-vessel-positions
#    (hourly, 23 past), archive-vessel-positions (03:41 UTC), prune-cross-source-samples (03:53 UTC).

# 2. Functions (the recorder now listens to aisstream; AISSTREAM_API_KEY is already a function secret;
#    supabase/config.toml already sets verify_jwt = false for both).
bunx supabase functions deploy record-vessel-positions
bunx supabase functions deploy collect-ship-frames

# 3. Checks.
bun scripts/regions/measure-coverage.ts <frames dir> <routes dir>      # coverage per region, exit 0 or 2
bun scripts/regions/cross-source.ts 24                                # after a day of collector runs, exit 0, 1 or 2
```

No new secret and no new Vault entry: the archive job reuses `recorder_function_url` and `recorder_secret`.

Check after a day, in the SQL editor: `select region_id, count(*) from public.vessel_positions p join public.cables c
on c.id = p.nearest_cable_id group by 1;`, the newest `public.recorder_runs.source_counts`, and the bucket
`position-archive` after 03:41 UTC.

### Rollback

Each migration's header carries its own rollback. In short, newest first:

1. `20261019500100`: unschedule `archive-vessel-positions`, restore the 14-day daily prune, drop the archive
   functions and `public.position_archives`; empty and delete the bucket in the dashboard.
2. `20261019500000`: drop the one-argument `ingest_vessel_positions`, re-run `20261017040000` and the
   `detection_inputs` of `20261018300000`, restore the 25 s recorder schedule, drop
   `public.cross_source_samples_since`, `public.cross_source_samples` and `recorder_runs.source_counts`. Redeploy the
   previous recorder and collector.
3. `20261019400700` to `20261019400100`: `delete from public.cables where region_id <> 'gulf-of-finland';` (after
   deleting the positions that reference those cables; their landing rows go with them, `on delete cascade`).
4. `20261019400000`: restore the unique key on name and kind, drop the three columns and `public.watched_regions`;
   restore the Gulf routes from `20261017020100` by name and kind.

## Open decisions

- **The fault-signal matcher's route read.** `public.fault_match_inputs` with routes (20261019300000) sends every
  cable's route: about 70 KB for the Gulf seed, 184 KB with the eight regions. A filter by region or by the signal's
  box keeps each call near its old size.

- **3 km or 10 km.** 3 km misses the Yi Peng 3 drag; 10 km keeps it but needs the detector every 60 minutes
  (`DETECTION_INTERVAL_MS` in `src/lib/detection/assess-fleet.ts` and the `detect-anchor-drag` schedule) to stay
  inside 5 GB of egress.
- **aisstream tracks of alerts.** The 2-day rule removes the positions behind an aisstream-region detection after 47
  hours; the detection row with its evidence and anchor path stays. Keeping such tracks needs aisstream's permission.
- **Coverage.** Five of the eight regions get 0 to 1 position a minute from aisstream. BarentsWatch (Norway) and an
  AISHub membership would change that; both need Andy.
